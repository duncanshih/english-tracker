## Communication Review

### Conversation Timeline

Greeting → Houston commute → unclear response → interview nerves → weekend → movie → unclear title response → associate closes

### 1. The commute topic broke because the response was not understandable

After the associate asked:

> “How was your commute in?”

the response was:

> “그...”

The associate answered “Nice,” which does not logically match the response. This shows that the message was not successfully understood. The larger problem was that the learner did not repair the misunderstanding. Once the associate moved on, the commute topic disappeared.

What should be done differently:

- Complete the answer with one clear fact: “It was pretty smooth. There wasn’t much traffic.”
- If the response was not recognized, repair immediately: “Sorry, I meant the commute was pretty smooth.”
- Do not allow an obviously mismatched response such as “Nice” to pass without clarification.

### 2. The learner added a useful feeling but did not hand the conversation back

The response:

> “I’m a little bit nervous, but I think I will be good.”

contains more than a one-word answer. It adds both a feeling and an expectation. This is an improvement over giving only “I’m nervous.”

However, it still leaves the associate responsible for continuing the exchange. A stronger version would add a small return:

> “I’m a little nervous, but I think I’ll be okay. Is it usually pretty busy here in the mornings?”

The goal is not to ask a question after every sentence. The goal is to make sure the other person has an easy next move.

### 3. The weekend question kept the conversation alive, but the transition was abrupt

After:

> “You’ll be fine.”

the learner asked:

> “How’s your weekend?”

This successfully started a new topic. It demonstrates that the learner can take initiative instead of waiting for the associate to rescue the conversation.

The weakness is the transition. The question shifts directly from interview nerves to the weekend without acknowledging the previous comment. A smoother handoff would be:

> “Thanks. How was your weekend?”

This adds only one word, but it makes the topic change feel connected rather than mechanical.

### 4. The movie topic became ambiguous because the key information was split across two turns

The learner first said:

> “I went to watch her movie.”

The word “her” has no clear person to refer to in the transcript. The associate therefore could not know which movie or person was meant.

The learner then answered:

> “Odyssey.”

The associate interpreted this as a random movie rather than clearly recognizing it as the title. The learner did not correct that interpretation, and the conversation ended.

The more effective structure is to give the activity and title together:

> “I went to see a movie called *Odyssey*.”

Then add one concrete reaction:

> “It was pretty interesting.”

That one sentence would remove the pronoun ambiguity, identify the title, and give the other person something natural to respond to.

### 5. Conversation handoffs

Successful handoffs:

- “How’s it going?”
- “How’s your weekend?”

Weak or failed handoffs:

- “그...” — not understood and not repaired.
- “I went to watch her movie.” — ambiguous reference.
- “Odyssey.” — too short to correct the associate’s interpretation or develop the topic.

The conversation survived the first two weak moments only because the associate supplied a new direction. It ended after the final weak handoff.

## Grammar, Usage & Pronunciation

### Grammar

#### Past-time weekend question

**Original**

> “How’s your weekend?”

**Corrected**

> “How was your weekend?”

**Rule**

Use the past tense when asking about a weekend that has already finished.

**Drill examples**

- How was traffic this morning?
- How was the movie?

### Usage

#### Expected interview performance

**Original**

> “I think I will be good.”

**Natural version**

> “I think I’ll be okay.”

**Why**

“Be good” can describe behavior or general ability; “be okay,” “be fine,” or “feel prepared” more naturally describe how someone expects an upcoming interview to go.

**Nearby alternatives**

- I think I’ll be fine.
- I feel pretty prepared.

#### Movie activity

**Original**

> “I went to watch her movie.”

**Natural version**

> “I went to see a movie.”

**Why**

“Go see a movie” is the standard conversational expression. “Her movie” requires a previously identified woman, which the transcript does not provide.

**Nearby alternatives**

- I saw a movie over the weekend.
- I went to see *Odyssey*.
