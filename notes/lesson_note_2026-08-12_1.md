> “Yeah How- how about your email”

You did not simply answer that the laptop was fixed; you redirected attention to the other person and reopened the earlier email topic. You did the same later with:

> “When, when are you gonna get off work”

These are useful workplace-small-talk behaviors because they prevent the interaction from becoming an interview in which one person only answers questions.

**Keep doing:** after giving your own answer, either add a concrete detail or return one related question.

### 2. Several answers contained the fact but not the expansion

For example:

> “I will resolve the update”

The partner had asked whether you sign off on updated numbers or pass them along. Your answer communicates that you handle the update, but it leaves the exact responsibility unclear. A more conversational response would be:

> “Usually I handle the update myself and make sure the numbers are consistent before we send the file out.”

That gives the partner something specific to react to: ownership, checking consistency, or sending the file.

A similar issue appeared here:

> “uh- finance, financial analyst accountant. Those type of role”

The categories are understandable, but the answer becomes easier to continue if you add one preference:

> “Mostly financial analyst and accounting roles. I’m especially interested in positions where I can do some analysis instead of purely transactional accounting.”

The key improvement is not “longer answers.” It is **one useful detail**.

A slightly more natural reusable version is:

## Grammar, Usage & Pronunciation

### Grammar

#### 1. Past tense for completed career experience

**Original**

> “I work as an auditor at Deloitte for three years”

**Corrected**

> “I worked as an auditor at Deloitte for three years.”

Use simple past for a completed period of employment.

**Drill examples**

- “I worked in audit for three years before moving into valuation.”
- “The technician fixed my laptop the same day.”

#### 2. Past tense after “later”

**Original**

> “Later I move into valuation.”

**Corrected**

> “Later, I moved into valuation.”

A completed career transition requires the past tense.

**Drill examples**

- “Later, I came to the U.S. for graduate school.”
- “After that, I moved into financial modeling.”

#### 3. Articles with singular countable roles and degrees

**Original**

> “I started my career as auditor at Deloitte”

**Corrected**

> “I started my career as an auditor at Deloitte.”

**Original**

> “Currently I'm looking for financial analyst role”

**Corrected**

> “Currently, I’m looking for a financial analyst role.”

Use **a/an** before a singular countable job title when it is not a proper title attached to a name.

**Drill examples**

- “I’m looking for an accounting role.”
- “I worked as an auditor before graduate school.”

#### 4. Plural nouns for general categories or multiple projects

**Original**

> “business valuation project”

**Corrected**

> “business valuation projects”

**Original**

> “support business decision”

**Corrected**

> “support business decisions”

Use the plural when referring generally to multiple projects or decisions.

**Drill examples**

- “I worked on several valuation projects.”
- “Analysts support business decisions with financial data.”

### Usage

#### “My computer cracked”

**Natural version:** “My computer stopped charging.”

“Cracked” normally suggests physical damage such as a cracked screen. Your later explanation showed that the actual problem was charging.

Nearby alternatives:
- “My laptop suddenly stopped charging.”
- “I had a charging issue with my laptop yesterday.”

#### “I can easily repair my laptop”

**Natural version:** “I could get my laptop repaired pretty easily.”

Because another person repaired it, **get something repaired** is more accurate than saying you repaired it yourself.

#### “I hate client changing the number”

**Natural version:** “I hate it when clients change the numbers.”

This is the normal conversational pattern for something that repeatedly annoys you.

Nearby alternative:
- “It’s always a pain when clients change the numbers at the last minute.”

#### “I will resolve the update”

**Natural version:** “I handle the updates.”

“Handle” naturally describes taking responsibility for revisions; “resolve” is more commonly used with a problem, issue, dispute, or discrepancy.

#### “pick out my girlfriend”

**Natural version:** “pick up my girlfriend.”

“Pick up” means collect someone by car; “pick out” means choose something from several options.

#### “Those type of role”

**Natural version:** “Those types of roles.”

Nearby alternative:
- “Those kinds of roles.”

#### “foundation of financial reporting”

**Natural version:** “foundation in financial reporting.”

The standard collocation is **a foundation in** a field or skill area.
