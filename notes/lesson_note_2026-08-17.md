# Grammar, Usage & Pronunciation

## Grammar

### 1. “I’m thinking” + plan

**Original**

> “I'm thinking I'm thinking going to New Orleans”

**Corrected**

“I’m thinking of going to New Orleans.”

**Rule:** Use **think of/about + -ing** when considering an activity.

Drills:

- “I’m thinking of cooking salmon tonight.”
- “I’m thinking about taking a short walk after work.”

### 2. Singular/plural and articles

**Original**

> “it costs. 4 dollar”

**Corrected**

“It costs four dollars.”

**Rule:** Use a plural count noun after numbers greater than one.

Drills:

- “The drive takes thirty minutes.”
- “The toll costs four dollars each way.”

### 3. Negative construction

**Original**

> “I still not understand”

**Corrected**

“I still don’t understand.”

**Rule:** In the simple present, use **do/does + not + base verb**.

Drills:

- “I still don’t understand that phrase.”
- “I don’t usually take the toll road.”

## Usage

### “from my home to office”

Natural version: “from home to the office.”

“Home” normally does not need “my” in this expression, while “office” usually takes “the” when referring to your workplace.

Nearby alternatives:

- “My commute is about thirty minutes.”
- “It takes me about thirty minutes to get to the office.”

### “bear the heat”

Your wording:

> “it was so hard for me to bear the heat in Houston”

This is grammatically possible, but in casual American conversation, “handle” is more natural:

“It’s really hard for me to handle the Houston heat.”

Also natural:

“I’m still not used to the Houston heat.”

### “a couple of coffee”

Natural version: “a couple of coffees” if you mean two servings/cups, or “a couple cups of coffee.”

### “Do you believe Ghost”

Natural version: “Do you believe in ghosts?”

With beliefs about existence, **believe in** is the normal expression.

### “Do you believe the existence of God”

Natural version: “Do you believe God exists?” or “Do you believe in God?”

The first asks specifically about existence; the second is the ordinary conversational form.

### “do you have heard ghost story”

Natural version: “Have you heard any ghost stories?”

This is a useful ready-made chunk; do not build it with “do + have heard.”
