For example:

This does not require a long answer. One extra piece of information is enough.

## Grammar, Usage & Pronunciation

### Grammar

#### 1. Questions after “do you know / understand”

**Original**

> “I don't quite understand what are you talking”

**Corrected**

> “I don't quite understand what you're talking about.”

After an embedded question, use statement word order: **what you are**, not **what are you**.

**Drill**

- “I don't know what you're planning for Christmas.”
- “Do you know which stores are open on the 26th?”

#### 2. Present perfect / past participle for a current result

**Original**

> “Traffic I stuck in the traffic jam”

**Corrected**

> “I'm stuck in traffic.”

Use **be + stuck** to describe your current situation.

**Drill**

- “I'm stuck at a red light.”
- “We're stuck in traffic near downtown.”

#### 3. Countable nouns

**Original**

> “Just some call”

**Corrected**

> “Just a couple of calls.”

Use a plural count noun after “some,” or use “a couple of.”

**Drill**

- “I had a couple of meetings.”
- “I made a few calls this afternoon.”

#### 4. “A 10 days”

**Original**

> “Like a 10 days”

**Corrected**

> “Like 10 days.” / “About 10 days.”

Do not use **a** before a plural time expression.

**Drill**

- “I'm going for about 10 days.”
- “I'll probably stay for two weeks.”

### Usage

#### 1. “Take it easy” vs. a quiet workday

**Original**

> “nothing special, just take it easy”

**Natural version**

> “Nothing special — it's been a pretty easy day.”

“Take it easy” usually describes deliberately relaxing or telling someone not to work too hard; for workload, “a pretty easy/quiet day” is more natural.

Alternatives:

- “It's been pretty quiet.”
- “Nothing too crazy today.”

#### 2. “Go exercising / take a show”

**Original**

> “I Go- exercising”

> “Go take a show. Go take a walk”

**Natural version**

> “I'm going to work out, take a shower, and go for a walk.”

Common collocations are **work out**, **take a shower**, and **go for a walk**.

#### 3. “Traffic light is not work”

**Original**

> “it's traffic light, it's not work”

**Natural version**

> “The traffic light isn't working.”

For equipment or infrastructure, “isn't working” is the usual phrase.

Alternatives:

- “The light is out.”
- “The traffic signal isn't working.”

#### 4. “What do you do for unwinding?”

**Original**

> “What do you Do for unwinding”

**Natural version**

> “What do you do to unwind?”

“To unwind” is the standard expression for relaxing after work.

Alternatives:

- “How do you usually unwind after work?”
- “What do you usually do to relax?”

#### 5. “Worth to visit”

**Original**

> “What uh worth to visit in Munich”

**Natural version**

> “What's worth visiting in Munich?”

Use **worth + -ing**, not **worth to + verb**.

Alternatives:

- “What should I see in Munich?”
- “What are the must-see places in Munich?”

2. **Clarification pattern:** Practice: “Sorry, what do you mean by ___?” → “Oh, got it — so you mean ___?”
3. **Transition replay:** Practice connecting two ordinary topics with “Speaking of…,” “By the way…,” or “That reminds me…,” without adding more than one bridge sentence.
