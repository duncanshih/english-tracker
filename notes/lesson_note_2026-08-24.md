## Grammar, Usage & Pronunciation

### Grammar

#### 1. Verb form after describing completed activities

**Original**

> "Grab a coffee and doing some chore"

**Corrected**

> "I grabbed a coffee and did some chores."

Use parallel past-tense verbs for activities you already completed.

**Drill examples** - "I washed the sheets and made dinner." - "I grabbed
an iced latte and ran a few errands."

#### 2. Count nouns and articles

**Original**

> "It's uh usual dinner"

**Corrected**

> "It's a usual dinner for me."\
> More naturally: "It's pretty normal for me."

A singular count noun such as *dinner* may need an article depending on
the construction, although conversational English often avoids the issue
with a simpler phrase.

**Drill examples** - "It's a pretty normal meal for me." - "I usually
have a high-protein dinner."

#### 3. "I don't want there to be..."

**Original**

> "I don't want there's too much smoke"

**Corrected**

> "I don't want there to be too much smoke."

After *want*, use *there to be* when expressing that you do not want a
situation to exist.

**Drill examples** - "I don't want there to be too much oil in the
pan." - "I don't want there to be too much dressing on the salad."

#### 4. Adjective pattern with *too*

**Original**

> "It is really little too rare"

**Corrected**

> "It's a little too rare."

Use *a little too + adjective*.

**Drill examples** - "The asparagus is a little too tough." - "The
dressing is a little too salty."

### Usage

#### "Ice latte" → "iced latte"

**Original**

*Iced latte* is the standard menu and conversational expression.

Nearby alternatives: "an iced latte," "a latte over ice."

**Original**

> "is really hard to chewy"

**Natural version**

> "It's really hard to chew."

*Chew* is the verb after *to*. *Chewy* is an adjective: "It's really
chewy."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Original**

> "the stems are a kind of woody"

**Natural version**

> "The stems are kind of woody."

Here *kind of* means "somewhat," so it does not take *a*.

Nearby alternatives: "The stems are pretty woody." / "The stems are a
little tough."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### "Repeat again" → "repeat that"

**Original**

> "can you repeat again"

**Natural version**

> "Could you repeat that?"

*Repeat* already contains the meaning of "again."

Nearby alternatives: "Could you say that again?" / "One more time?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### "Replace fries with salad" → "get a salad instead of fries"

**Original**

> "I would like uh replace fries with salad"

**Natural version**

> "Can I get a salad instead of fries?"

*Replace* is understandable, but *instead of* is the easiest everyday
restaurant phrasing.

The **instead of** version is the best one to retrieve quickly in live
conversation.
