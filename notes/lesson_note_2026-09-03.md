## Grammar, Usage & Pronunciation

### Grammar

#### 1. Degree/major construction

**Original:** "I graduated from Soochow University majoring Accounting"

**Corrected:** "I graduated from Soochow University with a degree in
accounting."

Use **major in + subject** or **a degree in + subject**.

Drills: - "I earned my master's degree in finance and accounting." - "I
majored in accounting at Soochow University."

#### 2. Experience/exposure construction

**Original:** "I had a opportunity to exposure to financial reporting"

**Corrected:** "I had the opportunity to gain exposure to financial
reporting."

Use **have an opportunity to + verb** or **have exposure to + noun**.

Drills: - "I had the opportunity to work with manufacturing clients." -
"I gained exposure to inventory counts and internal controls."

#### 3. Plural agreement

**Original:** "those company" / "those decision" / "those two
experience"

**Corrected:** "those companies" / "those decisions" / "those two
experiences"

After **those** or a number greater than one, use a plural count noun.

Drills: - "Those companies operated in different industries." - "Those
two internships strengthened my analytical skills."

#### 4. Past tense when describing completed work

**Original:** "I review 20 ... appraisal report"

**Corrected:** "I reviewed around 20 appraisal reports."

Use the simple past for completed internship responsibilities.

Drills: - "I challenged key valuation assumptions." - "I identified
several issues and escalated them to my senior."

### Usage

#### 1. "More close to the number"

**Original:** "to be more close to the number"

**Natural:** "to get closer to the numbers" / "to work closer to the
business"

"Closer to the numbers" is the natural comparative expression; "closer
to the business" is often even stronger when explaining a move from
audit into an operating role.

#### 2. "Rigor in number"

**Original:** "Rigour rigor in number"

**Natural:** "rigor with numbers" / "a disciplined approach to financial
analysis"

The preposition **with** fits the idea of being careful and disciplined
when working with numbers.

#### 3. "Good for shape the professional skills"

**Original:** "your company Uh is very good for shape the the
professional skills"

**Natural:** "it would be a strong environment to build my
cost-management skills."

This makes the company-to-career connection directly and avoids the
translated construction "good for shape."

#### 4. "Very potential platform for me to growth"

**Original:** "a very potential platform for for me to growth"

**Natural:** "a strong platform for me to grow" / "a strong environment
for professional growth."

Use **grow** as the verb and **growth** as the noun.

### 3. Clarifying unfamiliar casual English

Useful lines:

-   "Sorry, what do you mean by 'go-to'?"
-   "You mean my usual order?"
-   "Sorry, could you say that another way?"

The goal is to clarify quickly and then return to the conversation.

### 4. Explaining valuation work

Use a three-step frame:

**Responsibility → Method → Scale/result**

"I reviewed investment-property valuations for financial reporting. I
challenged assumptions such as discount rates, growth rates, and rents
and compared them with market data. Over three months, I reviewed around
20 reports covering roughly \$30 million in assets."

Reusable partner line from the conversation:

> "Can you tell me more about the valuation work you did at Deloitte?"

This is a useful cue to practice expanding one résumé item without
retelling your whole background.

### 5. Why this role / company

Role frame:

**What I already bring → What I want to deepen → Why this job connects
them**

Company frame:

**Industry/company fact → why it matters to me → connection to this
role**

Reusable partner line:

> "Why does that appeal to you?"

Use this as a reminder that an interviewer often wants the personal
connection after hearing a factual description of the role.
