## 4. Communication Review

### A. Conversation handoffs

- “What about you?”
- “Have you eaten already?”
- “What do you think about the weather today?”
- “What shows do you watch lately?”

These questions kept the conversation moving. However, **“What about you?”** appeared several times. It works, but you can vary it:

- “How about your weekend?”
- “Do you usually meal-prep?”
- “Have you watched anything good lately?”
- “What do you normally do when it’s this hot?”

### B. Topic transitions

> “Speaking of Stranger Things…”

That was natural and connected the previous topic to your personal experience.

Other useful transitions:

- “That reminds me…”
- “By the way…”
- “On a different note…”
- “Since we’re talking about shows…”

- “Sorry, what do you mean by ‘go-to burrito’?”
- “Could you say that one more time?”
- “Do you mean what I usually put inside it?”

- “I—I’ve eaten…”

## 6. Grammar and Usage

### 1. “I’ve eaten for my lunch”

Use:

- **I ate lunch.**
- **I’ve already eaten lunch.**
- **I already had lunch.**

“I’ve eaten for my lunch” is not a natural construction.

### 2. Eat soup vs. drink soup

Usually say:

- **I had some soup.**
- **I ate a bowl of soup.**

You may say **“drink soup”** when emphasizing drinking directly from a cup, but **“have soup”** is the safest everyday expression.

### 3. “Two bread”

**Bread** is normally uncountable.

- two slices of bread
- two pieces of bread
- some bread

Not:

- two breads
- two slice of breads

### 4. “Instant noodle”

Use the plural when discussing the food generally:

- instant noodles
- ramen

Example:

> “Sometimes I eat instant noodles.”

### 5. “Put into a fridge”

Use an object after **put**:

> “Put it in the fridge.”

### 6. “Put it into air fryer”

Use an article:

> “Put it in the air fryer.”

### 7. “Heat for five minutes”

More complete:

> “Heat it for five minutes.”

### 8. “And down!”

The intended word was probably:

> “And done!”

Natural expression:

> “Five minutes later, it’s ready.”

### 9. Contains vs. has

**Contains** is grammatically correct, but it sounds formal when describing food.

Formal:

> “The burrito contains ground beef.”

Conversational:

> “The burrito has ground beef.”

### 10. “Mexican mix cheese”

Use:

- Mexican-blend cheese
- a Mexican cheese blend
- shredded Mexican-blend cheese

### 11. “Upcoming weekends”

For the immediately approaching weekend:

> “this weekend”  
> “the upcoming weekend”

**Upcoming weekends** refers to multiple future weekends.

### 12. “What shows do you watch lately?”

Use present perfect continuous for recent activity:

> “What shows have you been watching lately?”

### 13. “They gonna protect”

Use:

> “They’re going to protect…”  
> “Their job is to protect…”  
> “The agency protects…”

### 14. “Drug in campus”

Use:

> “drugs on campus”

We normally say **on campus**, not **in campus**.

### 15. “How do this agent”

Plural subject:

> “How do these agents solve the problems?”

Singular subject:

> “How does this agent solve the problem?”

### 16. “The story is talking about”

Natural alternatives:

- “The story is about…”
- “The story follows…”
- “The show deals with…”
- “The series explores…”

### 17. “In this March”

Use:

> “this March”  
> “in March”

Not:

> “in this March”

### 18. “The effect is so awesome”

For 聲光效果:

> “The sound and lighting effects were amazing.”

Because you are discussing a past experience, use **were**, not **are**.

### 19. 前傳

The English word is:

> **prequel**

Example:

> “The stage show is a prequel to the series.”

## 9. Conversation Partner Lines You Can Reuse

These are useful responses and follow-up questions from the conversation. You can reuse them when making small talk with coworkers, interviewers, or friends.

### 1. Responding to someone’s lunch

> “That sounds cozy.”

適合對方提到湯、咖啡或簡單的食物時使用。

> “Sounds simple, but good.”

適合對方吃得很簡單時。

> “Do you usually have something light like that for lunch, or something heavier?”

可以自然地延續午餐話題。

**Shorter version:**

> “Do you usually eat a light lunch?”

### 2. Asking about someone’s usual food

> “What’s in your go-to burrito?”

意思是：

> 你平常最常吃的 burrito 裡面有什麼？

**Similar questions:**

> “What do you usually put in it?”

> “What’s your usual order?”

> “What’s your go-to lunch?”

**Go-to** means the option you regularly choose because it is dependable or convenient.

### 3. Reacting to food ingredients

> “That’s a solid combo.”

意思是這個搭配很好、很完整。

> “A little smoky, a little creamy. Sounds great.”

適合形容 chipotle、cheese 或其他味道的組合。

> “Can’t forget the cheese. That ties it all together.”

**That ties it all together** 表示某個食材讓整體變得更完整。

**Easier version:**

> “The cheese makes it even better.”

### 4. Talking about weekend plans

> “Got anything fun planned?”

非常自然的週末 small talk 問法。

> “Are you just going to take it easy?”

**Take it easy** 表示放鬆、不安排太多事情。

You can combine them:

> “Got anything fun planned, or are you just going to take it easy?”

**Other useful versions:**

> “What are you up to this weekend?”

> “Do you have any plans for the weekend?”

### 5. Responding to someone’s movie plan

> “That could be a nice weekend activity.”

> “A movie sounds like a good way to escape the heat.”

> “You can just sit back and relax for a couple of hours.”

**Sit back and relax** 表示坐著放鬆、享受當下。

### 6. Talking about simple personal plans

> “I’ll probably just catch up on rest.”

**Catch up on rest** 表示最近休息不夠，所以要補眠或多休息。

> “Maybe I’ll go for a walk if the weather is nice.”

> “Nothing too wild.”

**Nothing too wild** 表示沒有什麼特別瘋狂或盛大的安排。

Full response:

> “I’ll probably just catch up on rest and maybe go for a walk. Nothing too wild.”

### 7. Talking about hot weather

> “Stay hydrated.”

> “Take it easy during the hottest part of the day.”

> “A movie might be the perfect choice in this weather.”

> “Maybe you could go for a walk later, after it cools down.”

**After it cools down** 表示等天氣或溫度降低之後。

### 8. Suggesting small-talk topics

> “Weekend plans, hobbies, and shows you’ve watched lately are all easy topics.”

> “You could also ask if they’ve been to any good restaurants lately.”

Useful questions:

> “Have you watched anything good lately?”

> “Have you been to any good restaurants recently?”

> “What have you been up to outside of work?”

> “Do you have any plans for the weekend?”

### 9. Reacting to a show someone describes

> “That sounds intense.”

> “That sounds really interesting.”

> “I can see why it pulled you in.”

**It pulled you in** 表示故事很吸引人，讓你想繼續看下去。

> “Did it feel more like a drama or an action show?”

這是一個很好的 follow-up question，因為它不是只能回答 yes 或 no。

**Similar questions:**

> “What did you like most about it?”

> “Was it more serious or entertaining?”

> “Would you recommend it?”

### 10. Reacting to an exciting personal experience

> “That must have been wild.”

表示那個體驗一定非常震撼或刺激。

> “Seeing Vecna onstage must have been incredible.”

> “The lighting and sound effects must have made it feel really immersive.”

**Immersive** 表示讓人很有身歷其境的感覺。

Useful follow-up questions:

> “What was the best part?”

> “Was it similar to the Netflix series?”

> “Would you see it again?”

> “How close were you to the stage?”
