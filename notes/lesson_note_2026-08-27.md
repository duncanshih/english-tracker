# Grammar, Usage & Pronunciation

## Grammar

### Pattern 1: adjective subject — “I’m tired” vs. “today is tired”

**Original**

> “Oh, today is so tired”

**Corrected**

> “Oh, I’m so tired today.”

**Rule:** Use **tired** for the person experiencing fatigue; use **tiring** for the thing causing fatigue.

**Drills**

- “I’m exhausted today because I’ve been thinking about the interview.”
- “Interview preparation can be really tiring.”

### Pattern 2: countability and articles

**Original**

> “I have a full-time experience at Deloitte”

**Corrected**

> “I have full-time experience at Deloitte.”

**Rule:** **Experience** is normally uncountable when referring generally to professional experience.

**Drills**

- “I have three years of audit experience.”
- “I have experience with variance analysis.”

### Pattern 3: verb form after “to”

**Original**

> “Before take a sleep”

**Corrected**

> “Before going to sleep.”

**Rule:** After **before**, use a noun or an **-ing** form when no full clause follows.

**Drills**

- “I want to take a short nap before going to sleep.”
- “I’ll review my bullet points before going to bed.”

## Usage

### “set me apart” → “set myself apart”

**Original**

> “how do I set me apart?”

**Natural version**

> “How do I set myself apart?”

Because the subject and object refer to the same person, the reflexive form **myself** is required.

Nearby alternative:

> “How can I stand out from the other candidates?”

### “take effort” → “put in the effort”

**Original**

> “I just don't want to take Uh. effort to memorize it”

**Natural version**

> “I just don’t want to put in the effort to memorize it.”

**Put in the effort** is the standard collocation.

Nearby alternatives:

- “I don’t have the energy to memorize it.”
- “I don’t want to force myself to memorize everything.”

### “declined” → “rejected / not get the job”

**Original**

> “I don't want to be declined”

**Natural version**

> “I don’t want to be rejected.”

For a job outcome, a candidate is usually **rejected**, or more conversationally, **doesn’t get the job**.

Nearby alternative:

> “I’m scared I won’t get the job.”

### “trapped at Houston” → “trapped in Houston”

**Original**

> “I'm trapped at Houston.”

**Natural version**

> “I feel trapped in Houston.”

Cities normally take **in**.

### “take a sleep” → “go to sleep / go to bed”

**Original**

> “Before take a sleep”

**Natural version**

> “before I go to sleep” / “before I go to bed”

English normally uses **go to sleep**, **go to bed**, or **take a nap**, rather than “take a sleep.”

### 6. Useful conversation-partner lines to reuse

From the actual conversation:

> “What’s on your mind right now?”

Use when someone seems distracted or bothered and you want to give them space to explain.

> “What did you have in mind for dinner?”

Use when discussing evening plans or food; **have in mind** is a useful way to ask what someone is considering.

> “That sounds really wearing, especially when you're already tired.”

Use **wearing** for something that gradually drains your energy. An easier version is: “That sounds exhausting.”

> “What’s the hardest part about it tonight?”

Use when someone has described a problem and you want to understand the specific issue rather than immediately giving advice.

### Replay 1 — opening small talk

**Transcript**

> Learner: “next Thursday”

↓

**Problem**

The answer is correct but conversationally thin.

↓

**Better Response**

> “Next Thursday. So I’ve got about a week to get ready, which is good, but I’m already overthinking it a little.”

↓

**Why It Works**

You still answer directly, but one extra detail gives the partner several natural directions: preparation, nerves, timing, or the interview itself.

### Replay 3 — successful reciprocal question

**Transcript**

> Learner: “Yeah So how's your How's your wh- wha- What are you gonna eat for dinner”

↓

**Problem**

The conversational behavior is good, but the wording becomes tangled before reaching the question.

↓

**Better Response**

> “Yeah. What are you having for dinner?”

↓

**Why It Works**

This preserves the strongest part of the original turn—the handoff—while removing unnecessary setup. Short reciprocal questions are especially useful in small talk.
