### 2. Abstract explanations became difficult when too many ideas were packed into one turn

The clearest example was:

> “Speaking of twists, don’t you think recently people get addicted to short videos like TikTok or Reels? So people are hard to focus on the long video, like a film or a movie or a video that exceeds ten minutes or fifteen minutes or twenty minutes. So it’s hard for me to focus on those type of video.”

**What happened:** The learner had a clear point—short-form content may make long-form content harder to watch—but tried to express the trend, examples, time ranges, and personal experience in one extended construction.

**Why it became hard to follow:** The sentence kept reopening instead of reaching a clean endpoint. The listener had to infer the central claim while also processing several corrections.

**What to do differently:** Use a three-part structure:

1. General observation: “I think short-form videos are changing our attention spans.”
2. Personal example: “After scrolling through Reels, a one-hour episode feels like a big commitment.”
3. Handoff: “Have you noticed that too?”

This structure would preserve the learner’s idea while making it easier for the partner to respond.
